Why This Pivot

The Skills Transfer More Directly Than You'd Think

For three years, I was the person Agile teams turned to when a roadmap had to become reality. As a Scrum Master and Business Analyst on a high-traffic, defect-prone application team at Bank of America, I ran the ceremonies, prioritized the backlog, and translated stakeholder asks into shippable increments across 30+ production deployments. I'm now a full-time MSBA student at Wake Forest, sharpening the data side of my decision-making before stepping into product.

I'm not asking anyone to take a leap of faith on an unfamiliar discipline. Backlog prioritization, cross-functional facilitation, requirements gathering, stakeholder alignment, and risk management are the same muscles that make a strong Product Manager — I was just using them from the delivery side of the table. I already operated at the edge of that shift: I led the initiative to bring LLM-powered tooling into my team's documentation workflows at Bank of America, improving efficiency, accuracy, and data transparency — the kind of AI-fluent instinct product organizations are increasingly hiring for (see Industry Lens below).

What I'm building toward: less running someone else's roadmap, more owning outcomes.

Industry Lens

Where I Fit Into the Shift in Product Management

Four things reshaping product management in 2026 — and how my background already lines up.

AI Is Changing What "PM Work" Means

Product teams are moving from manually operating dashboards to governing AI agent workflows — evaluating and monitoring automated processes rather than running them by hand.

My angle: I lived this shift early — leading the rollout of LLM-powered tooling into my team's documentation and QA workflows at Bank of America to improve accuracy and transparency, before "AI governance" became a standard PM skill.

Outcomes Over Output

Organizations are replacing feature-velocity metrics with outcome reviews conducted months after launch — assessing adoption and impact, not just ship counts.

My angle: My UAT/QA ownership and post-deployment risk management on 30+ production releases is exactly this discipline — I already track whether a change actually worked, not just whether it shipped.

The Generalist Middle Is Collapsing

The PM role is splitting into "Builder-PMs" (technical, ship prototypes themselves) and "Integrator-PMs" (high-EQ, own cross-functional alignment) — specialization now commands a real market premium.

My angle: I'm positioning as an Integrator-PM with real Builder instincts — cross-functional facilitation backed by hands-on technical fluency (Python, SQL, JQL) and direct AI-tooling implementation experience.

Go-to-Market Is Now Core PM Work

Distribution and go-to-market literacy — onboarding, trial-to-paid conversion, in-app engagement — are now considered baseline PM responsibilities, not marketing's job alone.

My angle: This is my honest growth edge, and it's exactly why I'm pairing my delivery background with an MSBA focused on data-driven strategic decision-making rather than staying purely execution-focused.

Sources: Product School, "Product Management Trends: 11 Shifts Shaping 2026"; Userpilot, "6 Product Management Trends in 2026: The PM Role Is Splitting".
