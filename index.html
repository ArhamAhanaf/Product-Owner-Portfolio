Why This Pivot

The Skills Transfer More Directly Than You'd Think

For three years, I was the person Agile teams turned to when a roadmap had to become reality. As a Scrum Master and Business Analyst on a high-traffic, defect-prone application team at Bank of America, I ran the ceremonies, prioritized the backlog, and translated stakeholder asks into shippable increments across 30+ production deployments. I'm now a full-time MSBA student at Wake Forest, sharpening the data side of my decision-making before stepping into product.

I'm not asking anyone to take a leap of faith on an unfamiliar discipline. Backlog prioritization, cross-functional facilitation, requirements gathering, stakeholder alignment, and risk management are the same muscles that make a strong Product Manager; I was just using them from the delivery side of the table.

I already operated at the edge of that shift. Our dev leads and developers were losing significant time updating and completing documentation, work that isn't optional at a bank, since documentation accuracy is core to compliance and to catching problems before a deployment goes wrong. Every hour spent writing docs was an hour not spent coding, automating, or handling production issues. I led the fix: I fed Copilot our historical documentation, already validated by our Technical Product Managers, alongside the data from our upcoming deployments, and it began generating documentation accurate enough to pass TPM review consistently, a bar that's genuinely hard to clear even writing it by hand. That's the kind of AI-fluent instinct product organizations are increasingly hiring for (see Industry Lens below).

I also went back through five quarters of production incident tickets, tracked down and filled in missing values, and put new intake standards in place, including a required description template, standardized labels, and fields that had previously been optional. That cleanup fed directly into more accurate JQL queries and the Excel reports the business team relied on, and it meant every incident ticket finally looked consistent in Jira instead of each one following its own format.

What I'm building toward: less running someone else's roadmap, more owning outcomes.

Group Project · Multiple Linear Regression

A Regression Analysis of TikTok Virality Among 2024's Top Streamed Songs

The Dataset & Why TikTok

We built this on a public Kaggle dataset of the most-streamed Spotify songs of 2024 (4,601 tracks with matched Spotify, YouTube, Apple Music, and SiriusXM performance data). We chose TikTok views as our dependent variable specifically because of TikTok's emergence as one of the music industry's primary discovery and marketing engines. Labels increasingly treat a TikTok moment as the trigger for a song's broader success rather than a byproduct of it. We wanted to test that assumption directly: how well do a song's numbers on other platforms actually predict its TikTok performance, and vice versa? An early correlation check made the case for studying it separately: TikTok views were almost uncorrelated with Spotify streams (r = 0.04) or YouTube views (r = 0.02), while Spotify and YouTube moved together closely (r = 0.51). After removing rows with missing SiriusXM Spins (46% blank) and TikTok Views (21% blank), our clean working dataset came to 2,134 songs.

My Role

I helped source and clean the dataset, ran the platform-correlation checks that shaped our modeling strategy, and contributed to building and validating the regression models. I took sole ownership of translating the statistical output into the final business recommendations below; the hand-off from analysis to real-world decision-making is where I'm strongest and most confident.

Methodology

We built the model through backward elimination across three iterations. Model 1 started with nine predictors: Spotify streams, Spotify playlist count, Spotify popularity, YouTube likes, Apple Music playlist count, SiriusXM spins, an explicit-content flag, and two release-era dummies (2010–2020 and post-2020, benchmarked against pre-2010 releases). We dropped Apple Music playlist count first (p = 0.369, the weakest predictor), then Spotify popularity (p = 0.276) in Model 2, arriving at a seven-predictor final model in which every remaining variable was statistically significant at α = 0.05.

The Business Takeaway

Two findings ran against intuition. Spotify playlist adds and SiriusXM radio spins both correlated with fewer TikTok views, a sign that playlist- and radio-driven promotion targets a different audience and strategy than what actually goes viral on TikTok. Explicit tracks lost roughly 42 million views compared to clean versions, most likely reflecting TikTok's age-gating and content-moderation policies narrowing the reachable audience. And holding streaming performance constant, songs released after 2020 predicted fewer TikTok views than comparable pre-2010 tracks, a nostalgia effect where catalog and throwback songs consistently over-index on TikTok relative to their raw streaming numbers. The practical read for labels: chasing TikTok virality through playlist and radio saturation is the less reliable lever; genuine cross-platform engagement on Spotify and YouTube, and a willingness to push older catalog tracks, predicted TikTok performance far better. One honest limitation: our design only tests how other platforms predict TikTok, not the reverse, and about 29% of TikTok view variability is still unexplained, a reasonable target for a future model with variables like audio features or creator engagement.

Industry Lens

Where I Fit Into the Shift in Product Management

Four things reshaping product management in 2026, and how my background already lines up.

AI Is Changing What "PM Work" Means

Product teams are moving from manually operating dashboards to governing AI agent workflows, evaluating and monitoring automated processes rather than running them by hand.

My angle: I lived this shift early, leading the rollout of LLM-powered tooling into my team's documentation and QA workflows at Bank of America to improve accuracy and transparency, before "AI governance" became a standard PM skill.

Outcomes Over Output

Organizations are replacing feature-velocity metrics with outcome reviews conducted months after launch, assessing adoption and impact, not just ship counts.

My angle: My UAT/QA ownership and post-deployment risk management on 30+ production releases is exactly this discipline; I already track whether a change actually worked, not just whether it shipped.

The Generalist Middle Is Collapsing

The PM role is splitting into "Builder-PMs" (technical, ship prototypes themselves) and "Integrator-PMs" (high-EQ, own cross-functional alignment); specialization now commands a real market premium.

My angle: I'm positioning as an Integrator-PM with real Builder instincts, cross-functional facilitation backed by hands-on technical fluency (Python, SQL, JQL) and direct AI-tooling implementation experience.

Go-to-Market Is Now Core PM Work

Distribution and go-to-market literacy, including onboarding, trial-to-paid conversion, and in-app engagement, are now considered baseline PM responsibilities, not marketing's job alone.

My angle: This is my honest growth edge, and it's exactly why I'm pairing my delivery background with an MSBA focused on data-driven strategic decision-making rather than staying purely execution-focused.

Sources: Product School, "Product Management Trends: 11 Shifts Shaping 2026"; Userpilot, "6 Product Management Trends in 2026: The PM Role Is Splitting".
